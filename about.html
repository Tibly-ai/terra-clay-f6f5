About the studio

Clay, water, and a slow kind of patience.

Terra & Clay Ceramics started at a kitchen table in 2015, with a secondhand wheel and a bag of stoneware clay I could barely afford. I'd been making pots on and off for years, mostly for friends, until a neighbor asked to buy a set of bowls and I realized this could be more than a hobby.

Today the studio sits in a converted garage behind our house, where I throw, trim, and glaze every piece myself. There's no assembly line here. Some weeks I make forty mugs. Some weeks I spend three days on a single serving platter until the proportions feel right.

The work is unhurried on purpose. Clay tells you when it's ready, and I've learned it's easier to listen than to rush.

How a piece comes together

From raw clay to a finished piece, in four stages.

01

Throwing

Each form starts on the wheel, centered and pulled by hand from a single ball of stoneware clay. No molds, no casting.

02

Trimming and drying

Once leather hard, pieces are trimmed to their final shape and left to dry slowly over a week or more, so they don't crack.

03

Bisque firing

The first firing hardens the clay to bisqueware, around 1,830°F, preparing it to take glaze without dissolving.

04

Glazing and final firing

Glazes are mixed in small batches and applied by hand, then fired again to cone 6, around 2,232°F, where the surface turns to glass.
